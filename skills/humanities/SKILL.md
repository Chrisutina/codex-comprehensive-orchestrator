# Read texts with evidence and humility

Identify the exact work, author, edition/translation, language, and context. If the text is not provided, locate a reliable version and distinguish the author's words from later attribution or paraphrase.

## Analysis layers

1. literal meaning and structure;
2. imagery, diction, rhythm, syntax, voice, and rhetorical devices;
3. historical, philosophical, cultural, and intertextual context;
4. competing interpretations and tensions;
5. a reasoned personal or contemporary reading.

For quotations, verify wording and attribution before building an argument. Mark translations and explain when a word has multiple meanings. Do not turn an interpretation into a fact about authorial intent without evidence. For philosophical sayings, identify the original argument, scope, hidden premise, and whether the popular version is simplified or misattributed.

## Output

Give a concise thesis, supporting textual evidence, alternate readings, context, and an accessible explanation. Avoid empty praise and avoid pretending that ambiguity has one provable answer.
